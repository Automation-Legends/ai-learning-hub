# Beginner 3 — Verify Before You Trust

## Goal

By the end of this module, you will know how to review AI-generated content for factual accuracy, completeness, calculations, sources, and uncertainty before you use it.

## Why review matters

An AI assistant can generate language that sounds convincing even when information is incomplete, outdated, incorrectly combined, or entirely wrong. It may also provide citations that do not support the claim or that do not exist. The appropriate level of review depends on the consequence of being wrong.

> **The higher the impact, the higher the verification standard.** A brainstormed tagline needs a different review than a medical, financial, legal, safety, employment, or public-policy claim.

## A practical verification ladder

| Level | When it fits | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Low impact** | Brainstorming, personal drafts, simple reformatting | Read for obvious mistakes and make the final choice yourself. |
| **Medium impact** | Internal notes, learning materials, public copy | Check key claims against credible sources and ask a knowledgeable reviewer when needed. |
| **High impact** | Safety, health, money, legal rights, hiring, eligibility, or reputation | Use authoritative sources and qualified human review; do not rely on the AI output alone. |

## The SCOPE check

Use the following checklist before accepting a meaningful output.

| Check | Question | Example action |
|---|---|---|
| **S — Source** | Does each important claim have a real, relevant source? | Open the source and confirm it supports the exact statement. |
| **C — Calculation** | Are numbers, dates, units, and formulas correct? | Recalculate independently or use a trusted calculator or spreadsheet. |
| **O — Omission** | Is a key condition, exception, or perspective missing? | Compare against your original material or ask for limitations. |
| **P — Provenance** | What information did the output rely on? | Separate what you provided from what the AI generated. |
| **E — Escalation** | Does this require a person with authority or expertise? | Pause and route it for review before acting or publishing. |

## Activity — Audit an AI answer

Ask an AI assistant a question that contains at least three factual claims, such as a short explanation of a historical event, a comparison of two products, or a summary of a policy. Do not use a sensitive or consequential topic for this first exercise.

Then complete the following actions:

1. Highlight each factual claim and each number or date.
2. Check at least three claims using reliable, independent sources.
3. Mark claims as **confirmed**, **uncertain**, **incorrect**, or **unsupported**.
4. Ask the AI to revise its answer using only your confirmed information.
5. Write one sentence about what the original output got wrong or failed to explain.

Use the [evaluation sheet](../templates/evaluation-sheet.md) to record your review.

## Ask better follow-up questions

You can make review easier by asking the AI to expose uncertainty. Try prompts such as these.

```text
List the assumptions you made and label anything you are uncertain about.
```

```text
Provide a table with the claim, the source needed to verify it, and the confidence you have in the claim. Do not invent sources.
```

```text
Before answering, tell me which parts of this request need current information or expert review.
```

These prompts do not guarantee accuracy. They simply give you a better starting point for review.

## Completion check

You have completed this module when you have independently checked at least three claims from an AI response, corrected or qualified at least one result, and identified a situation where AI output should be escalated to human review.
