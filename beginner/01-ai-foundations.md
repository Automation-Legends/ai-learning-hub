# Beginner 1 — AI Foundations

## Goal

By the end of this module, you will understand what generative AI is useful for, recognize its important limits, and create personal rules for using it responsibly.

## The practical model

Generative AI produces new content in response to patterns in its input. It can be useful for drafting, summarizing, brainstorming, explaining, transforming text, organizing ideas, and helping you explore options. It does not automatically know whether its answer is correct, current, permitted, or appropriate for your context.

> **Treat an AI response as a fast first draft—not as proof.** The more important the decision or claim, the more carefully you should verify it.

## Good first use cases

| Task | How AI can help | Human responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Drafting | Create an outline, first draft, or alternative wording | Ensure the meaning, tone, and final message are correct |
| Learning | Explain a concept at different levels or generate practice questions | Check facts and use reliable learning sources |
| Organizing | Turn notes into categories, a checklist, or an action plan | Confirm priorities and omissions |
| Brainstorming | Generate options, questions, and counterarguments | Decide what is realistic, safe, and valuable |
| Text transformation | Summarize, reformat, translate, or simplify text | Check important details and preserve intended meaning |

## Limits to remember

AI can produce confident but inaccurate information, invent citations, misunderstand vague requests, miss important context, reinforce bias in its outputs, or generate advice that should be reviewed by a qualified professional. Current information may be incomplete or out of date. These limitations are not a reason to avoid AI; they are a reason to use it with clear boundaries and review.

## A responsible-use checklist

Before using AI for a task, ask yourself the following questions.

| Question | If the answer is “no” or “I am not sure” |
|---|---|
| Am I authorized to share this information with the tool? | Remove, anonymize, or do not use the information. |
| Can I explain the real goal in one sentence? | Clarify the task before prompting. |
| Will I independently verify meaningful claims or numbers? | Build verification into the workflow. |
| Is a human responsible for the final decision or publication? | Identify the reviewer before you begin. |
| Could the output affect safety, money, rights, health, or someone’s reputation? | Use additional expert review or a more appropriate process. |

## Activity — Write your personal AI use statement

Write five short rules you will follow when using AI. Include at least one rule about privacy, one about verification, one about accountability, and one about showing your own work or judgment. Keep the statement somewhere you can revisit before starting a new project.

### Example

> I will not enter private information unless I am authorized to do so. I will verify factual claims that influence a decision. I will use AI to create drafts and options, not to replace my responsibility for final work. I will disclose meaningful AI assistance when that transparency matters. I will pause and ask for help when a task has high stakes.

## Completion check

You have completed this module when you can name one task AI may help with, one limitation that could affect the task, and one concrete action you will take to manage that limitation.

## Reflection

What is one task in your work, study, or daily life that would become easier with a strong first draft? What information must stay out of the prompt?
