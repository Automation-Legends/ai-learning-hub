# Contributing to the Automation Legends AI Learning Hub

Thank you for helping make the learning hub more useful. Contributions should help members build practical skills, understand limits, and apply AI with appropriate human judgment. Small, well-tested improvements are welcome.

## Good contributions

Useful contributions include a clearer explanation, a new hands-on exercise, an improved template, a sanitized project example, a correction to an official resource link, or a lesson that helps learners evaluate an AI workflow. Every addition should be understandable without requiring a specific paid tool whenever possible.

| Contribution type | Expected standard |
|---|---|
| Lesson improvement | Explains a practical concept, includes an activity, and has a clear completion check |
| Template | Is reusable, tool-agnostic, and makes review or verification easier |
| Example project | Is sanitized, names the human-review step, and explains limitations as well as benefits |
| Resource link | Points to a credible primary source and states why it is useful |
| Correction | Identifies the affected page and provides a clear, verifiable fix |

## Before you contribute

Read the relevant learning path and confirm that your contribution fits the level of the learner. Use fictional, anonymized, or explicitly authorized material. Do not share credentials, personal data, confidential employer or client material, copyrighted content you do not have permission to publish, or instructions that bypass safety controls.

## Suggested workflow

1. Open an issue describing the learner problem you want to solve, unless the change is very small.
2. Fork the repository and create a focused branch.
3. Make the change in clear Markdown with working relative links.
4. Test all example prompts with sanitized input and explain any known limitations.
5. Add or update references for factual claims and external resources.
6. Open a pull request that explains the change, learner level, practical outcome, and any review needed.

## Content standards

Learning materials should be accurate, concise, practical, and respectful. State limitations plainly. Encourage members to verify material claims, protect sensitive information, and retain human accountability for significant decisions. Avoid implying that an AI output is automatically correct, unbiased, current, compliant, or appropriate for high-stakes use.

Use descriptive headings and tables when they help learners compare options or complete an activity. Prefer examples that demonstrate both a successful result and an exception or limitation. Keep examples tool-agnostic unless the lesson explicitly teaches a particular platform.

## Pull-request checklist

| Check | Confirm before requesting review |
|---|---|
| Scope | The contribution solves a specific learner need. |
| Clarity | The page has a clear goal, activity, and completion check. |
| Safety | Examples are sanitized and state relevant boundaries. |
| Accuracy | Important factual claims and resource links are verified. |
| Evaluation | Reusable prompts or workflows have representative test cases. |
| Accessibility | Language is clear, headings are descriptive, and tables remain readable. |
| Community fit | The contribution follows the [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md). |

## Review process

Maintainers may request edits for clarity, accuracy, safety, or scope. They may also suggest a narrower example or additional human-review safeguard. A contribution is ready to merge when it serves the intended learner, does not expose sensitive material, and improves the hub’s practical value.


## Share an AI tip, prompt, or resource

Community contributions do not need to be large lessons. A tested AI tip, a reusable prompt, a workflow pattern, or a well-chosen resource can save another member time and help them learn. Before sharing, make sure the contribution has a clear purpose, uses safe examples, and explains how a member should review the result.

### What to include

| Field | What to provide |
|---|---|
| **Title** | A specific description of the tip, such as “Turn approved meeting notes into an action-item table.” |
| **Who it helps** | The intended learner and the task or problem it addresses. |
| **Goal** | A one-sentence description of the result the tip is meant to produce. |
| **Steps or prompt** | Clear, copyable instructions with bracketed placeholders where appropriate. |
| **Example input** | A fictional, anonymized, or explicitly authorized example. |
| **Expected output** | A short description or example of what a useful result looks like. |
| **Review step** | What the user must check before relying on the output. |
| **Limits** | When the tip should not be used, what it cannot determine, or what remains uncertain. |
| **Source or attribution** | A credible original source for facts, tools, or ideas that are not your own. |

### Submission template

Copy this template into an issue, discussion, or pull request.

```text
## AI tip title
[Specific, practical title]

## Who this helps
[Audience and task]

## Goal
[One sentence]

## Steps or prompt
[Copyable instructions]

## Safe example input
[Anonymized, fictional, or authorized material]

## Expected output
[What a useful result looks like]

## What to verify
[Claims, dates, calculations, sources, decisions, or formatting to check]

## Limitations and boundaries
[When not to use this, data not to include, or where human review is required]

## Sources and attribution
[Links and credits]
```

### Tips that are ready to share

A strong contribution is specific enough to try, modest enough to verify, and honest about its limits. It should help another member understand both the opportunity and the responsibility involved in using AI.

| Ready to share | Needs revision before sharing |
|---|---|
| A prompt that turns authorized notes into a table and flags missing owners | A prompt that claims to make final decisions or guarantees a business result |
| A tool walkthrough with a clear first experiment and official source link | A tool recommendation based on an unverified feature, price, or outcome claim |
| A workflow example that identifies the human review step | A workflow that publishes, sends, approves, denies, or spends money without review |
| A sanitized screenshot or example with permission | A post containing credentials, private data, client information, or copyrighted material without permission |

### Attribution and responsible promotion

Credit the original author, source, or creator of any material you did not create. You may share a relevant product, service, template, or project, but it must be clearly disclosed when you have a financial interest or affiliation. Promotional material should still teach something useful and must not mislead members about results, availability, pricing, or access.

## Fast review path for small tips

Maintainers may accept a concise AI tip through an issue or discussion before turning it into a polished guide. The tip should include the goal, the safe example, a review step, and any source link. A maintainer may ask for clarification, additional testing, stronger privacy guidance, or a narrower claim before including it in the hub.
