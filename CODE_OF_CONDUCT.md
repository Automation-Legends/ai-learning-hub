# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

Automation Legends is committed to a welcoming, respectful, and constructive learning community. We want people with different backgrounds, experience levels, identities, and perspectives to be able to ask questions, share experiments, and improve their skills without harassment or intimidation.

## Expected behavior

Community members are expected to communicate in good faith, give feedback on ideas rather than people, respect privacy and intellectual property, and acknowledge uncertainty when sharing AI-generated or AI-assisted work. Help newer learners without ridicule, credit others for their contributions, and correct mistakes with evidence and care.

| We encourage | We do not allow |
|---|---|
| Respectful questions, thoughtful critique, and practical help | Harassment, discrimination, threats, or personal attacks |
| Honest discussion of failure modes and limitations | Misrepresentation of AI output as verified fact or original work when disclosure matters |
| Safe, authorized use of examples and data | Sharing credentials, private data, confidential information, or material without permission |
| Constructive disagreement supported by reasons | Trolling, bad-faith disruption, or repeated refusal to follow community boundaries |

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to repository issues, pull requests, discussions, shared learning materials, events, and other spaces where people represent or participate in the Automation Legends AI Learning Hub.

## Reporting concerns

If you experience or witness behavior that conflicts with this code, contact an Automation Legends organization maintainer privately through the GitHub organization. Include links, screenshots, or a concise description when it is safe to do so. Reports will be handled with discretion and reviewed as promptly as practical.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may clarify expectations, remove or edit content, close discussions, restrict participation, or take other appropriate action to protect the community. Actions will be proportionate to the behavior, context, and need to maintain a safe learning environment.

## Good-faith learning

AI systems can produce errors, harmful outputs, and misleading confidence. Members are encouraged to discuss these failures openly. A genuine mistake that is acknowledged and corrected is part of learning; repeated harmful conduct or refusal to respect clear boundaries is not.
