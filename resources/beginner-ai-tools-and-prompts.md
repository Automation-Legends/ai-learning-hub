# Beginner-Friendly AI Tools and Prompts

This resource helps new Automation Legends members choose one approachable tool, complete one useful experiment, and learn how to review the output. You do not need every tool on this list. Pick the tool that best fits your first task, read its current terms and privacy settings, and use non-sensitive material while learning.

> **Start with one tool and one real task.** The goal is not to collect AI accounts. The goal is to learn how clear instructions, safe inputs, and human review improve a useful piece of work.

## Choose a first tool

Availability, plan limits, privacy controls, and features can change. Use each provider’s official page for current details before connecting an account, uploading content, or relying on a feature.

| Tool | Good first use | First experiment | Important habit |
|---|---|---|---|
| [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/overview) | Everyday questions, learning, writing, planning, comparison, and brainstorming | Turn rough notes into a five-item weekly plan | Ask for a specific format and check each deadline or factual claim. [1] |
| [Claude](https://claude.ai/) | Writing, explanations, study support, ideation, and code-related questions | Ask for a plain-language explanation of a topic you are learning | Provide your experience level and ask it to mark uncertainty. [2] |
| [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com/) | General AI assistance and conversational exploration | Ask it to compare two approaches to a low-risk task in a table | Treat the response as a starting point; Google states that Gemini can make mistakes. [3] |
| [Gemini Notebook](https://notebooklm.google.com/) | Learning from sources you provide, including notes and documents | Upload approved study notes and create practice questions with citations | Use only sources you are authorized to share and open the cited source passages before relying on an answer. [4] |
| [Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/) | Research questions and discovering source material | Ask for a short research starting list on a non-sensitive topic | Open and inspect cited sources; a cited link does not automatically make a claim correct. [5] |
| [Canva AI](https://www.canva.com/canva-ai/) | Drafting and refining visual or written communication | Create a simple event flyer or social post from a factual brief | Review text, images, brand use, and rights before publishing; Canva notes that AI results can make mistakes. [6] |

## A simple tool-selection guide

| If your first goal is… | Start with… | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Understand a topic or improve everyday writing | ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini | A general assistant gives you a low-friction way to practice clear prompts. |
| Study from your own approved materials | Gemini Notebook | It is designed to work from sources you add and to surface citations. [4] |
| Explore a current question and find starting sources | Perplexity | It presents answers alongside sources to investigate. [5] |
| Make a visual post, flyer, or presentation outline | Canva AI | It supports conversational creation and editable design work. [6] |

## Before you begin

Use this five-question check before placing information in any AI tool.

| Question | If the answer is “no” or “not sure” |
|---|---|
| Am I authorized to share this information? | Do not upload it; remove, anonymize, or replace it with a fictional example. |
| Can I explain the task and desired result in one sentence? | Clarify the goal before writing a prompt. |
| Can I independently check important facts, dates, numbers, or sources? | Build verification into the task or choose a lower-risk experiment. |
| Do I know who will review the result before it is used or shared? | Name the reviewer or keep the task as a private draft. |
| Is the task low-risk enough for a first experiment? | Narrow the task or seek appropriate human expertise. |

## Ten starter prompts

Replace the text in brackets with your own non-sensitive details. After each prompt, read the result and make at least one improvement. That improvement is part of the learning.

### 1. Explain a topic simply

```text
Explain [topic] to someone with [my experience level].

Use a plain-language explanation, one real-world example, and a five-question self-check quiz.

If a claim depends on current information or needs a source, label it clearly so I can verify it.
```

**Use it for:** learning a concept, preparing for a conversation, or understanding unfamiliar terms.

### 2. Turn notes into a weekly plan

```text
Turn the notes below into a weekly plan.

Return a table with: priority, next action, owner, due date, and questions to confirm.

Use only the information I provide. Do not invent owners, dates, or tasks. Mark missing details as [confirm].

<notes>
[Paste non-sensitive notes here]
</notes>
```

**Use it for:** organizing personal, study, or approved project notes.

### 3. Improve a message without changing the facts

```text
Rewrite the message below for a [warm / professional / concise] tone.

Preserve every factual claim. Keep it under [number] words. Return a subject line and the revised message.

List anything that is unclear or needs my confirmation; do not fill gaps by guessing.

<message>
[Paste a non-sensitive draft here]
</message>
```

**Use it for:** emails, announcements, invitations, or personal drafts.

### 4. Create practice questions from approved notes

```text
Create ten practice questions using only the notes below.

Use six multiple-choice questions and four short-answer questions. Put the answer key after the questions and quote the supporting note for each answer.

If the notes do not contain enough information for an answer, write [not covered in the notes].

<notes>
[Paste approved study notes here]
</notes>
```

**Use it for:** study preparation. Check every answer against your original material.

### 5. Brainstorm options with trade-offs

```text
I need ideas for [goal]. My constraints are [time, audience, budget, resources, or format].

Give me five options in a table with: idea, expected benefit, likely challenge, first small step, and what I would need to verify.

Do not present estimates or outcomes as guaranteed.
```

**Use it for:** planning a project, event, content series, or personal experiment.

### 6. Prepare for a meeting or interview

```text
Help me prepare for a [meeting / interview / presentation] about [topic].

Ask me up to three clarifying questions first. Then create:
1. A one-minute opening;
2. Five likely questions or objections; and
3. A short list of facts I should confirm before the event.

Keep the content accurate to the information I provide and flag assumptions.
```

**Use it for:** confidence-building and structured preparation.

### 7. Convert feedback into themes

```text
Group the feedback below into themes.

Return a table with: theme, representative comments, possible action, and uncertainty or missing context.

Do not infer protected traits, personal motives, or facts that are not in the feedback. Use [needs review] when a comment is ambiguous.

<feedback>
[Paste anonymized and authorized feedback here]
</feedback>
```

**Use it for:** safely organizing approved survey responses or community feedback.

### 8. Create a research starting plan

```text
I want to research [question].

Create a research plan with:
1. The key sub-questions;
2. The kinds of primary or authoritative sources I should look for;
3. Search terms to try; and
4. A checklist for evaluating a source.

Do not state unverified conclusions. Treat this as a plan for my own research, not as a final answer.
```

**Use it for:** starting an investigation without mistaking AI output for the research itself.

### 9. Draft a community post

```text
Draft a community post inviting beginners to try [activity].

The audience is [who]. The goal is [what participants should learn or do]. Keep the tone welcoming and practical.

Include: a clear title, a three-step activity, a reminder not to share sensitive information, and one reflection question. Keep it under [number] words.
```

**Use it for:** posts, challenges, learning activities, or announcements.

### 10. Review a prompt and improve it

```text
Review this prompt for clarity, missing context, risky assumptions, and output format.

First, list what is already clear. Then identify the three most important improvements. Finally, rewrite the prompt using the headings: Goal, Context, Rules, Input, Output Format, and Quality Check.

<prompt>
[Paste my draft prompt here]
</prompt>
```

**Use it for:** improving how you ask for help before you start a larger task.

## The review step that matters

A helpful answer can still contain errors. Before using or sharing a meaningful result, run the SCOPE check from [Verify Before You Trust](../beginner/03-verify-before-you-trust.md).

| Check | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| **Source** | Does each important claim have a real, relevant source? |
| **Calculation** | Are numbers, dates, units, and formulas correct? |
| **Omission** | Is important context, an exception, or a different perspective missing? |
| **Provenance** | What did I provide, and what did the AI generate? |
| **Escalation** | Does a person with authority or expertise need to review this? |

## Share your first experiment

After trying one tool and one prompt, share a short, sanitized reflection in the community.

```text
Tool I tried: [tool]

Task I gave it: [one sentence]

What helped: [one useful part of the result]

What I checked or changed: [one review step]

What I will try next: [one improvement]
```

Your reflection is more valuable than a perfect output. It helps the community learn which approaches are useful, where they fail, and how to improve them safely.
