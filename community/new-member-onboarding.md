# Welcome to the Automation Legends AI Learning Hub

## A message for new members

Welcome to **Automation Legends**. You are joining a community for people who want to use AI with curiosity, practical judgment, and respect for the people affected by the work. Whether you are opening an AI assistant for the first time or already building workflows, you belong here.

You do not need to know every tool, use technical language, or have a finished project to participate. Start with one real problem that matters to you. It might be planning your week, understanding a concept, organizing notes, drafting a message, exploring an idea, or improving a recurring task. We will help you turn that problem into a safe, useful experiment.

> **Our working principle:** AI can accelerate drafts, analysis, and learning. You remain responsible for facts, decisions, privacy, and the final work you share.

## Your first 30 minutes

| Step | What to do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Read the community standard** | Read the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) and the responsible-AI guidance in the [main hub](../README.md). | It establishes a respectful, safe learning environment. |
| **2. Choose a learning path** | Start with the [Beginner Path](../beginner/README.md) unless you already use AI regularly and can evaluate outputs reliably. | A clear sequence prevents tool overload. |
| **3. Introduce yourself** | Share your first name or preferred name, your goal, and one task you hope AI can help you improve. Do not share private information. | It helps other members point you toward the right resources. |
| **4. Pick one tool** | Choose one general AI assistant or one focused tool from the [Beginner Tools and Prompts](../resources/beginner-ai-tools-and-prompts.md) guide. | Learning one tool deeply is more useful than opening many accounts. |
| **5. Try one safe prompt** | Use a practice prompt with non-sensitive information. | You will gain a quick, low-risk success and learn how to improve a result. |

## Your first seven days

This guide is deliberately lightweight. Set aside 15–30 minutes a day, or complete the steps at your own pace during the week.

| Day | Focus | Action | Completion signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Day 1 — Start** | Meet the community | Post a short introduction and choose one real task to improve. | You have named a learning goal. |
| **Day 2 — Learn the basics** | Understand AI’s strengths and limits | Complete [AI Foundations](../beginner/01-ai-foundations.md). | You have written personal rules for privacy, verification, and accountability. |
| **Day 3 — Write a better prompt** | Practice clarity | Use the [Prompt Template](../templates/prompt-template.md) to improve one vague request. | You have compared a first prompt with a revised prompt. |
| **Day 4 — Check the output** | Build review habits | Complete the SCOPE verification activity in [Verify Before You Trust](../beginner/03-verify-before-you-trust.md). | You have confirmed, qualified, or corrected at least three claims. |
| **Day 5 — Join the discussion** | Learn with peers | Reply to the [first-week discussion prompt](first-week-discussion-and-poll.md) and vote in the poll. | You have shared one practical observation or question. |
| **Day 6 — Build something small** | Apply the learning | Complete a first draft of the [Your First AI Project](../beginner/04-first-ai-project.md). | You have documented one bounded AI-assisted workflow. |
| **Day 7 — Reflect and share** | Turn practice into progress | Use the [Project Showcase Template](../templates/project-showcase.md) to share a sanitized reflection. | You can explain what AI helped with and what still required human judgment. |

## How to introduce yourself

Copy, edit, and post this message in the community discussion area.

```text
Hello Automation Legends. I am [name or preferred name].

I am joining because I want to use AI to improve [a task, skill, or project].

My current experience level is [new to AI / learning the basics / already using AI regularly].

One question I have is: [question].

One boundary I will keep in mind is: [for example, “I will not paste private information into a tool without permission.”]
```

## How to get help

Good questions help the community give useful answers. When asking for help, explain the outcome you want, what you have tried, the kind of input you are working with, and any boundaries that matter. Remove confidential information and replace it with placeholders before posting.

| Instead of this | Try this |
|---|---|
| “My prompt does not work.” | “I want a five-item action list from these meeting notes. I asked for a summary, but it missed owners. How can I ask for a table that flags missing owners?” |
| “Which AI tool is best?” | “I want to turn my own study notes into practice questions. I need a tool that can work from sources I provide and make review easy. What should I try first?” |
| “Can AI do this for me?” | “Which step of this process could AI safely assist with, and where should I keep human review?” |

## Community practices that keep learning useful

| Practice | What it means here |
|---|---|
| **Share safely** | Do not post private, sensitive, proprietary, or unauthorized material. Use fictional or anonymized examples when practicing. |
| **Verify meaningfully** | Check important facts, calculations, sources, instructions, and summaries before acting or publishing. |
| **Give helpful feedback** | Explain what worked, what was unclear, and what you would try next. Critique the work, not the person. |
| **Show the process** | When you share a result, include the goal, the prompt or workflow approach, the review step, and the limitation you found. |
| **Celebrate progress** | A small completed experiment, a clear question, or a corrected mistake is valuable learning. |

## Your next milestone

After completing your first week, you should be able to write a clear prompt, spot when output needs checking, and describe a small task where AI can help without taking over your judgment. Continue through the [Beginner Path](../beginner/README.md), then move to the [Intermediate Path](../intermediate/README.md) when you are ready to make workflows repeatable.

We are glad you are here. Start small, learn in public when it is safe, and help the next member who is one step behind you.
