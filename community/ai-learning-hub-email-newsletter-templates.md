# AI Learning Hub Email Newsletter Templates

Use these templates to invite **opted-in** subscribers to the Automation Legends AI Learning Hub. Replace every bracketed field before sending. Keep the sender identity, subscription preferences, and unsubscribe information required by your email service provider and applicable requirements. Do not promise outcomes that the hub cannot substantiate.

> **Email principle:** Give subscribers a useful reason to open the message even if they do not join or click today. The hub invitation should be a clear next step, not pressure.

## Email quality checklist

| Before sending | Check |
|---|---|
| Audience | The recipient has opted in to receive this kind of communication. |
| Claim accuracy | Every linked resource, event, feature, price, or membership benefit is available and described accurately. |
| Clear value | The email offers a practical lesson, template, or action—not only an invitation. |
| One main action | The primary link or reply request is clear and easy to find. |
| Accessibility | The subject line is concise, headings are readable, and link text describes the destination. |
| Privacy | You do not place subscriber information, credentials, or private member details in the email or linked examples. |
| Respect | The message avoids pressure, fabricated urgency, and misleading claims. |
| Compliance | The sender identity, required footer content, and unsubscribe controls are present. |

## Suggested follow-up sequence

| Timing | Purpose | Template to use |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Introduce the hub and its purpose | Template 1 |
| Day 3–5 | Help beginners take a first action | Template 2 or Template 4 |
| Day 10–14 | Invite experienced members to build a workflow | Template 3 |
| Monthly | Share useful updates and reinforce the learning habit | Template 5 |

## Metrics to learn from, not to manipulate

Review delivery, opens, clicks, replies, unsubscribes, and the type of questions subscribers ask. Use these signals to improve clarity and relevance, not to pressure people. The most useful long-term measure is whether a subscriber found a resource, completed a small practice activity, or returned to learn again.
