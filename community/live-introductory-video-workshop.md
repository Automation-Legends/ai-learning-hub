# Live Introductory Video Workshop — Welcome to the AI Learning Hub

## Workshop purpose

This 45-minute live video workshop welcomes new members to Automation Legends, explains how the AI Learning Hub works, gives every participant one small, safe practice activity, and invites them to choose a realistic first-week action. The session is designed for people with mixed experience levels.

> **Facilitator promise:** Participants will leave knowing where to start, how to practice safely, and how to ask the community for useful help.

## At a glance

| Item | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Audience** | New Automation Legends members, including people new to AI and members returning after a break |
| **Length** | 45 minutes, including 10 minutes for questions |
| **Format** | Live video with chat, a poll, screen share, and optional breakout pairs |
| **Main outcome** | Each participant chooses one low-risk task, writes one improved prompt, and identifies one verification action |
| **Materials** | The [AI Learning Hub](https://github.com/Automation-Legends/ai-learning-hub), [New-Member Onboarding Guide](new-member-onboarding.md), [Prompt Template](../templates/prompt-template.md), and [First-Week Discussion and Poll](first-week-discussion-and-poll.md) |
| **Accessibility** | Enable captions, share links in chat, speak slide text aloud, pause for written participation, and provide a recording or written recap when appropriate |

## Facilitator preparation

Complete the following before the session.

- [ ] Open the AI Learning Hub and the resources listed above in separate browser tabs.
- [ ] Prepare one fictional, non-sensitive example task for the prompt activity.
- [ ] Set up the poll: “What is your biggest AI learning priority right now?”
- [ ] Enable captions and identify a co-host or moderator to welcome people and collect questions.
- [ ] Decide how members will access the recording, recap, and first-week discussion afterward.
- [ ] Confirm that any demo uses a safe tool, a test account, and no private information.
- [ ] Prepare the three chat links: hub, onboarding guide, and first-week discussion.

## Full facilitation script

### 0:00–0:05 — Welcome and community norms

**Say:**

> Welcome to Automation Legends and the AI Learning Hub. We are glad you are here. This community is for people who want to use AI for practical work, learning, and projects while keeping human judgment, privacy, and respect at the center.
>
> You do not need to be technical, and you do not need a perfect project idea today. Please use the chat to ask questions, but do not post credentials, private documents, client information, or anything you are not authorized to share. We will use fictional examples for the practice activity.
>
> By the end of this workshop, you will have one task you want AI to help with, one improved prompt, and one clear next step in the hub.

**Do:** Share the agenda slide or a simple agenda in chat. Ask participants to add their first name or preferred name and one word describing what brought them to the community.

**Chat prompt:**

```text
Welcome. In the chat, share your name or preferred name and one word for what brought you here: learning, prompts, workflow, automation, project, community, or something else.
```

### 0:05–0:10 — What the hub is for

**Say:**

> The hub is organized around a simple cycle: **Learn, Practice, Verify, and Share**.
>
> First, you learn one practical idea. Then you practice it on a real, low-risk task. Next, you verify important claims, numbers, sources, and decisions. Finally, you share a sanitized reflection so that other members can learn from what worked and what did not.
>
> The Beginner Path covers AI foundations, better prompting, verification, and a first project. The Intermediate Path moves into reliable workflows, automation, APIs, and evaluation. The Advanced Path introduces bounded custom agents and vector databases. You do not need to rush through the paths. The right start is the path that fits your current experience.

**Do:** Screen share the [hub landing page](https://github.com/Automation-Legends/ai-learning-hub) and point to Beginner, Intermediate, and Advanced paths.

**Ask:**

> If you are not sure where to start, choose Beginner. Is anyone here deciding between the paths?

### 0:10–0:15 — Poll and introductions

**Say:**

> I am going to launch a quick poll. Your answer helps us choose future challenges and workshops.

**Launch poll question:**

> What is your biggest AI learning priority right now?

**Options:**

1. Writing better prompts
2. Learning and research
3. Saving time on routine tasks
4. Creating content or designs
5. Understanding AI tools
6. Building automations or projects

**Say after voting:**

> Thank you. There are no wrong answers. The goal is not to master everything. It is to choose one useful starting point.

**Chat prompt:**

```text
Finish this sentence: “If AI could help me with one thing this week, it would be ________.”
```

Read two or three responses aloud, with permission, and connect them to relevant hub resources.

### 0:15–0:25 — Live prompt activity

**Say:**

> We will now practice the skill that transfers across almost every AI tool: giving a clear request.
>
> Here is a vague task: “Help me organize my week.” A model may give a generic answer because it does not know the goal, the context, the constraints, or the output format.
>
> Let us improve it together.

**Screen-share or paste this example:**

```text
Goal: Turn these approved weekly notes into a plan.

Context: I am planning a one-week community learning schedule.

Rules: Use only the notes I provide. Do not invent owners, dates, or tasks. Mark missing details as [confirm].

Output format: Return a table with priority, next action, owner, due date, and question to confirm.

Quality check: List assumptions and missing information after the table.
```

**Say:**

> Notice that the prompt does not make AI correct by itself. It makes the output easier to check. It tells the system what it may use, what it must not invent, and how to show uncertainty.

**Do:** Invite participants to write a single sentence in chat describing their own task. Choose one non-sensitive example and help the group add goal, context, rules, and format.

**Optional breakout prompt:**

```text
In pairs, spend three minutes turning one vague request into a prompt with:
1. A goal;
2. Context;
3. A boundary or rule; and
4. A useful output format.
```

### 0:25–0:32 — Review and verification

**Say:**

> A better prompt is only the beginning. The next habit is verification.
>
> Use the SCOPE check: **Source, Calculation, Omission, Provenance, and Escalation**. Ask: What source supports this? Are the dates and numbers right? What might be missing? What came from me versus the AI? Does someone with authority or expertise need to review this?
>
> For a simple draft, you may only need a quick read. For anything involving money, health, legal rights, safety, access to services, or someone’s reputation, the review standard must be much higher.

**Do:** Show the [Verify Before You Trust](../beginner/03-verify-before-you-trust.md) page and the [Evaluation Sheet](../templates/evaluation-sheet.md).

**Ask:**

> In the prompt we just improved, what is one detail you would check before using the output?

### 0:32–0:37 — Community projects, badges, and the capstone

**Say:**

> When you are ready, the hub moves from individual practice to community building.
>
> In Week 2, members design an automation workflow with APIs, structured output, and a human review queue. In Week 3, advanced members design bounded custom agents that use approved knowledge and show evidence. In Week 4, beginners and intermediate builders collaborate on a Community Resource Review Assistant. The point is not to automate a final decision. The point is to prepare a transparent draft that a moderator can review.
>
> We also recognize progress through the Week 1 Explorer, Week 2 Workflow Builder, and Learning Hub Pathfinder badges. Your evidence can be a safe reflection, a workflow canvas, or an evaluation summary. It does not need to be a polished product.

**Do:** Share links to the [badge system](week-1-and-2-completion-badges.md) and the [Week 4 capstone](week-04-collaborative-capstone.md).

### 0:37–0:45 — Questions and first-week commitment

**Say:**

> We have time for questions. You can ask about a tool, a task, a prompt, a workflow, or where to start. If your question needs private information to answer, keep the private details out and describe the situation in general terms.

Answer questions concisely and direct members to existing resources where possible.

**Close with:**

> Before you leave, choose one action for the next seven days. It can be as small as completing AI Foundations, improving one prompt, checking one AI output, or posting your first project idea.
>
> Add your commitment in the chat: “This week, I will ________.”
>
> Thank you for joining Automation Legends. Start small, verify what matters, share what you learn safely, and help the next member who is one step behind you.

## Facilitator reflection

After the workshop, record what participants asked for, where they hesitated, and which resources they opened. Use those observations to improve the next session. A successful welcome workshop does not need to answer every question; it should make the next safe learning action obvious.
